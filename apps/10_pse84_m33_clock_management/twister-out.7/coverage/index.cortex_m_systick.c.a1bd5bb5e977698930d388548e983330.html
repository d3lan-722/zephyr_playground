26 /* Minimum reload the driver will program, i.e. the closest-in timeout it can
27 * schedule. Two floors apply.
28 *
29 * Hardware: LOAD is programmed as (cycles - 1), and a LOAD of zero stops the
30 * counter (it reloads to zero and never makes another 1->0 transition, so no
31 * further interrupt), so the smallest usable value is two cycles (LOAD 1).
32 *
33 * Masking: it must also exceed the longest time the SysTick interrupt can stay
34 * masked. elapsed() reconstructs the current cycle count assuming at most one
35 * wrap of the counter has happened since it last ran; it cannot tell one wrap
36 * from several. With a tiny LOAD the counter wraps every few cycles, so if
37 * interrupts are then held off longer than that LOAD (a long critical section,
38 * or a higher-priority ISR that runs for a while) the counter wraps two or
39 * more times before the SysTick ISR can account for it. Each unaccounted wrap
40 * silently drops a full LOAD's worth of cycles: the software clock falls
41 * permanently behind real time and every pending timeout fires late by that
42 * much. A floor above the worst-case masking window guarantees at most one
43 * wrap between reads, which elapsed() does handle.
44 *
45 * That is a wall-clock budget, so express it as a fixed time converted to
46 * cycles at the actual frequency (k_us_to_cyc_ceil32() follows the runtime
47 * rate where the timer reports it), computed at init. A fixed cycle count is
48 * meaningless across clock rates: the old 1024-cycle floor is ~10 us at
49 * 100 MHz but ~31 ms (31 ticks!) on a 32 kHz SysTick. The tick rate is
50 * deliberately not involved; if the resulting value exceeds one tick on some
51 * clock, sub-tick timeouts are simply unavailable there.
52 *
53 * Keep it as small as correctness allows, not as large as a tick would
54 * permit: this is a floor for safe rescheduling, not a jitter control.
55 * Inflating it does trim the short-period tail, but it eats sub-tick headroom,
56 * and as it approaches CYC_PER_TICK the driver can no longer place a timeout
57 * inside the current tick, so the small per-ISR surplus is carried over and
58 * two ticks get announced at once (a double expiry). 10 us is under a cycle at
59 * 32 kHz, where the two-cycle hardware floor takes over; either way it stays
60 * far below CYC_PER_TICK.
61 *
62 * A device tree "zephyr,min-timeout-cycles" property still overrides the
63 * budget, subject to the same two-cycle hardware floor.
64 */
